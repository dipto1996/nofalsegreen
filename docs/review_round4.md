## 2. Remaining weaknesses, ranked

### W1. P3 (+0.427) is mostly a three-firm I-9 effect, and the bootstrap overstates its precision. *(b) for the analysis, (a) for the wording, (c) optional.*

**Location.**
- Abstract ("raise the false-green rate by 0.427").
- §1, finding 1 ("the largest cause we found").
- §6.1 (cluster definition).
- §6.2 ("15 of the 18 clusters").

**Why it matters.** From `exploratory.json`, under the dashboard schema:

| Rule family | Pairs | Firms | Plain FGR | Proxy FGR | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I-9 | 99 | 3 | 0.023 | 0.686 | **+0.66** |
| Wage | 81 | 9 | 0.723 | 0.842 | **+0.12** |

The pair-weighted average reproduces about 0.42. So the pooled P3 is roughly 80% an I-9
effect, and that effect is observed in **three firms**.

The cluster bootstrap resamples the 18 (firm, rule) clusters as exchangeable. But the three
I-9 clusters of one firm share one withheld export, one roster and one set of proxy values.
In effect there are 12 independent units, not 18. The "15 of 18 clusters positive" count
inherits the same double-counting: 9 of those clusters are 3 firms counted three times.

Under the offered schema the picture is reversed and healthier: I-9 +0.19 and wage +0.26.
That is why P4 is the more general result.

**Cheapest convincing fix (b).**
- Rerun the declared estimator with **firm-level clusters (12)**. Label it a sensitivity
  analysis, not a re-test.
- Add a **sign-flip permutation test** on cluster-level paired differences. With 12 or 18
  units it is exact and has no 1/2,000 floor.
- Report P3 and P4 **split by rule family** in the body. One extra row pair in Table 6
  would do it.
- Report the cluster signs **by firm** (for example, "I-9: 3 of 3 firms; wage: k of 9").

**Wording (a).**
- Say "+0.427, of which the I-9 rules (3 firms) contribute most; +0.12 on the wage rule
  (9 firms)".
- Drop "the largest cause we found". The comparison is across contrasts with different
  baselines and different firm support.

**Optional (c).** Add 6 more seeds selected to withhold the I-9 export. Run both arms under
offered and dashboard, with the 7 models: about 6 firms × 2 arms × 2 schemas × 4 batches × 7
models ≈ 670 calls. This would turn "3 firms" into 9 and is the one experiment that would make
the P3 headline robust.

### W2. "Detection does not imply safety" is contradicted in spirit by Figure 6. *(a), plus a one-line (b).*

**Location.**
- Abstract.
- §1, finding 3.
- §6.3, "A recall-based evaluation would rank these models highest without revealing this."
- Figure 6.

**Why it matters.** Across the 7 Study 3 models, plain-firm recall and proxy-firm FGR (both
under the offered schema) are strongly *aligned*. The Spearman correlation of recall with
(−FGR) is ≈ **0.67** (from Table 16). The two lowest-recall models (Gemma, Qwen3) have the two
highest FGRs. A recall-based ranking would therefore put roughly the same models on top that
an FGR ranking would.

What the data actually show is narrower: **no model is safe in absolute terms, however high
its recall.** Even the best has FGR ≥ 0.16 on proxy firms and ≥ 0.10 on plain firms. The
current wording invites a reader to take the claim as orthogonality, and Figure 6 visibly
refutes that.

**Fix.**
- Rephrase as "High recall does not bring the false-green rate near zero".
- Report the rank correlation in one clause.
- Delete the sentence about recall-based ranking, or rewrite it as "would report these models
  as near-perfect".

### W3. Finding 1 mixes the two arms. *(a)*

**Location.** §1, finding 3; abstract ("models that detect nearly every violation still call
16% to 36%").

**Why it matters.** The recall threshold ("at least 95%") is measured on **plain** firms, but
the FGR is measured on **proxy** firms. On proxy firms gpt-oss-120b's recall is 0.920
(Table 16), so "four models that detect at least 95%" is false for the arm in which the FGR
is reported.

**Fix.** Use the proxy-arm recall for all four models, which gives "at least 92%", or say "on
plain firms … and on the same firms with proxy columns". Fix Figure 6's axis note to match.

### W4. The "mechanism" reading leans on self-reported citations. *(a); optional (c).*

**Location.**
- §6.3, "Models cite the proxy columns".
- In particular: "The I-9 status column … was never cited, so what models take as evidence is
  a date or an amount … not a status flag."

**Why it matters.** The evidence field is the model's own account of its reasoning. Its
faithfulness to what actually caused the verdict is not established. `i9Status = Complete` can
shift the verdict while the model cites `onboardingCompletedDate`. The citation table shows
association. It cannot show that the status flag is inert.

**Fix (a).** Change "so what models take as evidence is" to "models *cite* dates and amounts,
never the status flag". Add one sentence noting that cited fields are self-reports.

**Minimal experiment (c), if the claim is kept.** A single-column ablation on the 3 (or W1's 9)
I-9 firms, under the offered schema only, with the 7 models. Three variants:
1. `i9Status` only;
2. `onboardingCompletedDate` only;
3. `workAuthValidThrough` only.

That is 3 variants × 3 firms × 4 batches × 7 models ≈ 250 calls. If variant 1 leaves FGR near
the plain level, the status-flag claim stands.

### W5. Reverification plus `workAuthValidThrough` is the weakest "false green", and it is not separated out. *(b)*

**Location.** §3.2; §6.3; Table 7 (`workAuthValidThrough`: 33 of 134 offered-schema I-9
false greens, 88 of 462 under the dashboard schema).

**Why it matters.** For the reverification rule, a roster saying authorisation is valid until
a date after the evaluation date implies that no reverification is yet due. A practitioner
would call that "not triggered". This is not the same move as reading an onboarding date as a
Section 2 date. Pooling the three I-9 rules hides which ones carry the proxy effect.

**Fix.**
- Add a per-rule breakdown (S1, S2 and reverification timeliness) of the plain→proxy change and
  of the cited fields. The data exist.
- If reverification carries much of it, say so in §3.2's "proxy-acceptance rate" paragraph.

### W6. In the wage family, the "proxy" duplicates a value the plain arm already shows. *(a)*

**Location.** §4.2 ("a salary of record set at the LCA offered wage"); §6.3.

**Why it matters.** On wage firms the proxy arm adds `annualBaseSalary`, which equals the LCA
offered wage that plain firms already show. The wage proxy effect (+0.26 offered) therefore
comes from **re-presenting an existing number in a roster column**, not from new
evidence-like information. That points to salience or location, which is interesting in
itself, but it is not the same mechanism as the I-9 case.

**Fix.** State it in one sentence in §6.3. Do not describe the two families' effects as the
same phenomenon.

### W7. The reporting rule in §3.3 is gameable by `always_fail`. *(a)*

**Location.** §3.3, "The false-green rate must be reported beside violation recall; neither is
meaningful alone"; Table 2.

**Why it matters.** `always_fail` scores FGR 0.000 and recall 1.000, which is perfect on both
named metrics (Table 2). Figure 7's caption admits that `always_fail` sits on the oracle's
point. The stated reporting requirement does not prevent that trivial system from looking
perfect.

**Fix.** Require FGR alongside recall **and** the false-alarm rate, or abstention recall. Note
that the anchor firm has no controls, so on it only abstention recall separates `always_fail`
from the oracle.

### W8. "Separable" and "larger on proxy firms" overstate the schema results. *(a)*

**Location.**
- §1, finding 2 ("in two separable parts … Both effects are larger on firms with proxy
  columns").
- §6.3 ("So the schema and the proxy columns compound").

**Why it matters.**
- *Not separable.* Each step bundles changes: P1 removes two verdicts and three sentences; P2
  redefines both `pass` and `fail` and removes a sentence. "Separable" is precisely what the
  design cannot claim.
- *"Larger" is not established.*
  - The only declared interaction (S3) was null: +0.048, p = 0.10.
  - The abstention interaction was never tested.
  - "Larger" is a comparison of point estimates from an exploratory plain-arm analysis.
- *The abstract omits part of P2.* It says "redefining `pass` … (with one warning sentence
  dropped)" and leaves out the redefinition of `fail`.

**Fix.**
- Use "two sequential steps".
- Write "larger in point estimate on proxy firms (exploratory; the declared interaction test
  for the definition step was not significant)".
- Add "and `fail`" to the abstract.

### W9. §8 says the effects "replicate … across five developers", which is too strong for P2. *(a)*

**Location.** §8, "Is this only prompt sensitivity?"

**Why it matters.**
- P2 is significant for 1 of 7 models.
- On the anchor firm the abstention step was null for Gemini 3.1 Pro (−0.008).
- On Study 2 no contrast survived correction.

The pooled replication is real; per-model replication is not.

**A second problem.** "Involves no change to the prompt at all" is literally false: the
exports *are* the prompt.

**Fix.**
- Say "the pooled effects hold on fresh firms and in both developer groups".
- Say "no change to the instructions".

### W10. Figure 7 in the body puts contested anchor recall on an axis. *(a)*

**Location.** §7, Figure 7 (p. 13).

**Why it matters.** §4.4 says anchor recall is "only agreement with the engine", and the second
auditor supports only 7 of the 20 labels. Yet the main exploratory figure plots that recall as
its x-axis, with a "target" region and a "detects violations, but unsafe" region.

**Fix.** Move Figure 7 to the appendix, or replace it with an FGR-only dot plot (13 models ×
offered/dashboard). See §4 below.

### W11. The p-value is reported as a point value when it is a resolution limit. *(a)*

**Location.** Abstract; §6.2 ("Holm-adjusted p = 0.002 each").

**Why it matters.** 0.002 is 4 × the 1/2,000 floor. Writing "p = 0.002" implies precision that
does not exist.

**Fix.** Write "p ≤ 0.002 (the bootstrap's resolution)". Better, report the W1 permutation p.

### W12. The terminology in the released artifacts lags the paper. *(a)*

**Location.**
- `results.json` keys use `decoy` and "adjacent columns"; the paper says "proxy".
- The preregistration uses "adjacent-field substitution" and "decoy".
- Figure 7's legend says "abstention withheld (dashboard)", which reintroduces the "withheld"
  collision that round 3 flagged.

**Fix.**
- Add a one-line mapping in Appendix F or in the datasheet: decoy = proxy; `abstain_withheld` =
  dashboard.
- Relabel Figure 7.

### W13. Still open from round 3 and acknowledged, but these are the real ceiling. *(c)*

- **Prompt paraphrase.** Minimal experiment: 2 paraphrases each of the offered and dashboard
  menus, on the 12 proxy firms, with 3 models (one strong, one weak, one other-developer).
  That is about 2 × 2 × 12 × 4 × 3 ≈ 1,150 calls. It would let §8 make its "not only prompt
  sensitivity" argument empirically.
- **Closed frontier models.** One OpenAI and one Anthropic model on Study 3's offered and
  dashboard cells, both arms.
- **Counsel labels.** Two attorneys label a 100-pair sample of proxy-arm false greens: is
  `pass` defensible under the standard you would apply? This is the single item that moves
  the ICAIL/JURIX score, and it directly tests W5 and W6.

## 4. Presentation and visual quality

### 4.1 Global layout

- **The paper is not in a venue template.** It uses `article`, 11pt, with 1-inch margins, a
  `fancyhdr` running head, `parindent=0` and `parskip`. Reviewers notice this immediately, and
  page counting is impossible. Use `acl.sty` for ACL/EMNLP or `neurips_2026.sty` for D&B now,
  so that length is judged on the real page budget.
- **Tables are shrunk with `adjustbox`, and Table 8 (p. 12) is unreadable** when printed: about
  5pt. Never scale a table below `\footnotesize`. Split it, or move it to the appendix.
- **Floats drift from their text.**
  - Table 1 lands on p. 4, after its paragraph.
  - Table 5 (p. 8) is a five-column text table whose cells wrap to 4 to 6 lines.
  - Figure 6 and Table 8 share p. 12, ahead of their sections.

  Use `[t]` placement, and put each float on the page where it is first cited.
- **Two naming systems are mixed.** Appendix tables use code identifiers
  (`gemini-3.1-pro-preview`, `qwen3-235b-a22b-instruct-2507-maas`), while figures use display
  names. Use display names everywhere, and keep the API identifiers in one table in Appendix B.
- **Monospace identifiers break lines badly** in §7's bullets (p. 14) and in Table 7's caption.
  Shorten them (for example, `…business-expense`) or move them to a table.
- **Number formatting is inconsistent.** Figures use ".25"; text and tables use "0.25". Use the
  leading zero everywhere. Give rates the same number of decimals in the text (0.427) and in
  the figures (0.43 or 0.427, not both).
- **Colours change meaning between figures.**
  - Blue means "plain firms" in Figures 3, 5 and 6, "needs_evidence (correct)" in Figure 8, and
    "violation shown" in Figure 9.
  - Red/orange means "proxy" in Figures 3 and 5, but "pass (false green)" in Figure 8.

  Fix one semantic palette: red = `pass` on an unassessable pair, or the proxy arm; blue =
  correct abstention, or the plain arm; grey = not significant or reference. Keep it fixed
  across all figures.
- **Editorial text sits inside the plots:** "detects nearly every violation, still unsafe"
  (Figure 6), "the target" and "detects violations, but unsafe" (Figure 7). Top venues frown
  on this. Remove it, and let the caption carry the message.

## Bottom line

No new data are needed to fix the wording. Two zero-cost analyses would close the main
soundness gap:
1. firm-level clustering, or a permutation test;
2. per-family and per-I-9-rule splits of P3 and P4.

The cheapest experiment that would materially strengthen the paper is **more firms that
withhold the I-9 export** (W1, about 670 calls). The second is a single-column proxy ablation
(W4, about 250 calls).

Then cut the body to about 4,300 words in the venue template, with 5 figures and 3 tables.
Most of the cuts are the anchor-label story told three times, and tables that duplicate
adjacent text or figures.
